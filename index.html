Hearing Tests

Find yourself asking people to repeat themselves? A hearing test shows what you’re hearing today, so you don’t have to guess.

Your audiologist checks how well you hear a range of sounds and how well you understand speech. Then they go over the results with you and talk through your options and next steps.

What you get from the visit

  • A clear picture of your hearingResults for both sounds and speech, so you can see what may be behind what you’re noticing.
  • Results explained in plain termsTime to ask questions about the conversations and places where hearing feels harder.
  • A clearer next stepYou’ll talk through what the results mean for you, including whether hearing aids could help.

Common reasons people come in

  • Asking people to repeat themselves
  • Voices sound muffled or unclear
  • Trouble following conversation in noisy places
  • Turning the TV up louder than others prefer
2If hearing aids are right for you

Hearing Aid Fittings

Hearing aids work best when they’re chosen and set up for you. We offer hearing aids from multiple brands and match the options to your hearing results and the situations that matter most to you.

How we check the fit: Real Ear Measurement

With your hearing aid in place, your audiologist measures the sound inside your ear and uses those readings to fine-tune the settings. The result is a fitting tailored to your ears, rather than settings left at a general starting point.

What that means for you

  • Options matched to your dayRecommendations based on your hearing, your comfort, and where you want to hear better, from dinner conversation to TV dialogue.
  • Settings checked in your earReal Ear Measurement readings guide the fine-tuning, so adjustments are based on what’s reaching your ear.
  • Guidance for everyday useHelp with the practical parts, such as charging and connecting a compatible phone, so daily use feels easier to manage.
  • Adjustments as you settle inYour hearing aids can be fine-tuned or reprogrammed later, including if your hearing changes.

Questions before you call

Quick answers about your hearing test, choosing hearing aids, and coverage.

What happens during a hearing test?

Your audiologist checks how well you hear a range of sounds and how well you understand speech. Afterward, they go over your results with you, answer your questions, and talk through next steps. If hearing aids could help, that conversation includes your options.

Should I get tested if I’m not sure my hearing has changed?

Yes. Hearing changes often happen gradually, so they can be hard to judge on your own. A hearing test shows where your hearing stands today and can clarify what you’re noticing.

If your results are normal, they still give you a baseline to compare against in future tests.

How do you help me choose hearing aids?

Your audiologist starts with your hearing test results, then talks with you about the everyday situations where you want to hear better, such as conversations at home, phone calls, or the TV. Comfort and how you’d like to use your hearing aids day to day are part of the conversation too.

We offer hearing aids from multiple brands, so the options we discuss can be matched to your hearing and your routine.

Can my hearing aids be adjusted later?

Yes. Hearing aids can be fine-tuned or reprogrammed after your fitting, for example as you get used to them, or if a later hearing test shows your hearing has changed.

When you call, ask our team what follow-up visits are part of your hearing aid care.

Can you help me check my insurance coverage?

Yes. Our front-office team can help check your benefits. Coverage depends on your specific plan, so have your insurance information handy when you call 818-214-8397.
